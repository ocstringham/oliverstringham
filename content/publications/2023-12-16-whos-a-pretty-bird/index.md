## Abstract

The online pet bird trade can be a pathway for both conservation loss and biological invasion, but the traits that drive which species are most heavily traded are poorly understood. We analyzed roughly 14,000 online sales listings from a popular Australian marketplace, covering 235 parrot and songbird species. Non-native species made up 55% of traded parrots and 64% of traded songbirds, and the large majority of these (81% and 85%, respectively) face an extreme risk of establishing invasive populations if released. Traded abundance was best predicted by species' breeding and handling requirements, trade availability, and physical appearance and behavior — traits that can help prioritize species for monitoring and regulation.
