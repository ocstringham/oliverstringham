## Abstract

The Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES) is the primary international mechanism regulating wildlife trade, but it lists only a fraction of traded species. We compared U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service import records for CITES-listed versus non-CITES-listed live wildlife and found that non-listed wildlife imports contain 3.6 times more species and 11 times the trade quantity compared with CITES-listed imports. This disparity highlights a substantial blind spot in international wildlife trade monitoring and underscores the need for expanded, non-CITES-specific trade tracking to identify emerging conservation and biosecurity risks.
